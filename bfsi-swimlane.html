    Incident clocks

    A new kind of vendor: AI agents. In September 2026, Australia's government said an unreleased AI agent from a major developer had been accessing a government Medicare portal since June, and that the government was told only in September. The lesson above applies unchanged. If a provider's AI agent can reach your systems, your contract needs to say how fast they must tell you, because your regulator's clock may already be running.     What's coming

    Settlement is moving onto shared ledgers. Your operating rhythm will follow.

    Not yet daily reality for most BFSI GCCs, but the direction is clear, and it touches two phases of the map above.

    Phase 2, Daily operations

    The batch window thins out

    In September 2026, UK banks in a seven-bank project made their first payments to each other in tokenised deposits, with remortgage funds released automatically once the property transfer was confirmed. Settlement like this runs around the clock and reconciles as it goes. Cut-offs and end-of-day reconciliation, the rhythm many BFSI teams are built around, start to disappear.

    Phase 6, Incidents

    No overnight window to recover

    When settlement is instant and always on, there is no quiet period to repair a failed batch before customers notice. Continuity plans and incident runbooks written around business hours need rework before the parent's first live flow, not after it.

    People

    Retrain before the work goes

    Reconciliation and KYC processing roles are already shrinking. Centres that move those teams into exception handling, controls and data quality early keep the knowledge. Those that wait lose it along with the roles.
